What to Tell Dispatch

Give the street address and ZIP code, then describe where the water is coming from and how far it has spread. Say whether the water touches outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, and whether it looks clean or smells like sewage. Those answers decide which steps are safe while a crew is arranged.

If you can do so safely, shut off the main water valve before you call. If you smell gas or see sparking, phone the utility or 911 before us.

A 60 Second Checklist Before You Call

  • Close the main water valve if the water is coming from a supply line.
  • Stay out of standing water that may touch outlets, appliances or the electrical panel.
  • Take a few wide photos and a short video of the water and the damaged items.
  • Write down the street address, ZIP code and the best phone number to reach you.
  • Find your insurance policy number if it is easy to reach, but do not delay the call to look for it.

None of these steps should delay the call. If you cannot do them safely, call first and dispatch will walk you through what to do.

After the Call

Dispatch confirms what happens next and gives you what to expect on site. Keep your phone nearby, because the crew may call from a different number when they are close. Tell your insurer about the loss as soon as you can, and the state insurance division recommends reporting the loss promptly.

When Email Makes Sense

Email suits questions that are not urgent, such as asking which towns we cover, what a stage of the process means or where to find our service list. Anything involving active water, sewage, a smell of gas or a wet electrical panel belongs on the phone. A message can sit unread overnight, while a phone call reaches a person at once.
