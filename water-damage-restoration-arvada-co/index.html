What the City's Own Records Say About Arvada Flooding

The City of Arvada states that its floodplains took effect in 1974 and that most of the neighborhoods along the creeks, including Ralston Creek, were already built. In plain terms, many creekside homes were built before the mapping that now governs them. The city adds that everyone has flood risk, even outside the mapped floodplain.

The most recent named event in the city's own report is the flood of September 11 to 13, 2013. That report names Leyden Creek as the most severe, with Ralston Creek, Van Bibber Creek, Moon Gulch and the Croke Canal also affected. It also records sewer surcharges, including some homes along Leyden Creek and a few homes near 64th Avenue and Miller Street.

A sewer surcharge means the public sewer fills enough that wastewater pushes back toward homes, and that is how a flood becomes a basement sewage problem. If that is what happened, flood damage restoration explains the contaminated-water handling.

Sewer Lines, Sump Pumps and Who Owns What

Arvada's Utilities Department provides water and sewer service for most of the city, and wastewater is treated by Metro Water Recovery. The city's published guidance says the homeowner maintains the sewer service line from the house to the city main and that the city does not cut, clean or televise the residential portion. The Utilities Department's main and emergency line is 720-898-7070.

The city also states that discharging a sump pump into the sanitary sewer is prohibited, and that discharge should go to a landscaped surface. That matters for water damage, because a sump pump hose draining in the wrong place can overload the sewer line or put water back against the foundation.

Parts of Arvada are served by other districts, including Ralston Valley Water and Sanitation, Valley Water, Clear Creek Sanitation, North Table Mountain Water, Consolidated Mutual Water and Denver Water. Which one serves your address affects who you call about a main line problem.

Arvada Homes, From the 1945 Boom to Today

The city's history page describes a housing boom beginning around 1945 north of Ralston Road. Its 2024 housing assessment adds that the median year built for homes sold in 2022 and 2023 was 1977 and that about three quarters of the housing stock was built before 2000. Seventy-one percent of housing units are detached single-family homes.

Those ages point to aging water heaters, galvanized or older copper lines, original roofs and sump pumps near the end of their life. A 1977 house is now almost fifty years old, and the plumbing behind a finished basement wall has usually never been inspected.

If Water Is Still Spreading, Do This Before We Arrive

Stop the source

Turn the main valve off. If the water is rising from a drain, stop using water in the house.

Stay clear of power

Leave the breakers alone unless the panel is dry and within easy reach. Gas smell or sparking means phone the utility or 911.

Lift and protect

Raise valuables and small furniture, and place foil or blocks under wood legs on wet carpet.

Record the damage

Photograph the water and the damaged items before moving them, and contact your insurer.
