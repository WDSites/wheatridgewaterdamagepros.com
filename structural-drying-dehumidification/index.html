How Wet Buildings Actually Dry

This page covers the drying phase itself, the science and equipment that take a wet building back to normal. It assumes the water is already stopped and the bulk of it removed. For the first days of a loss, see water mitigation. Here the question is simply how moisture leaves a wall, a floor or a slab, and how a crew knows it is finished.

Drying is evaporation plus removal. Water in a material turns to vapor, the vapor enters the air, and the air has to be carried out or condensed. If any of those three steps stalls, the building stays wet.

Psychrometrics in Plain Words

Psychrometrics is the study of air and water vapor. Three ideas are enough. Air can hold only so much vapor, and warm air holds more than cold air. Relative humidity says how full the air is. And wet materials give up moisture only when the air around them is dry enough to take it.

Crews often work with grains per pound, a measure of the actual weight of vapor in the air, because it does not change with temperature the way relative humidity does.

Front Range Air Versus a Closed-Up House

Outdoors, Wheat Ridge air is usually dry. This semi-arid area dries a puddle on a patio quickly. Inside a sealed house the picture changes. Doors and windows are shut, the heat may be low, and the vapor from wet carpet, drywall and concrete has nowhere to go.

Safety Steps While Drying Equipment Runs

Drying equipment adds power draw and moving air to a home that already took water. A few habits keep the setup safe for the household.

  • Leave equipment plugged into the circuits the crew chose, and do not add extension cords or power strips.
  • Keep children and pets out of rooms with equipment and cords across the floor.
  • Do not switch off or relocate a machine to quiet the room. Call and describe the problem instead.
  • If a breaker trips or a cord feels hot, stop using that circuit and call dispatch.

The Equipment and What Each Piece Does

Drying equipment in a typical setup
EquipmentJob it doesWhere it fits
Air moversMove air across wet surfaces to speed evaporationFloors, wall bases, ceilings
LGR dehumidifiersPull vapor out of the air at warm, humid conditionsMost homes after a water loss
Desiccant dehumidifiersUse a drying wheel that works at low temperatures and low humidityCold spaces, crawl spaces, deep or stubborn drying
Injection or cavity dryingPush dry air into closed wall and floor cavitiesBehind baseboards, under cabinets, in subfloors
Air scrubbersFilter the air in the work zoneOdor, dust or contaminated jobs

LGR stands for low grain refrigerant. The unit chills incoming air so vapor condenses, then reheats it. It works best when the room is warm and holds a lot of vapor.

A desiccant unit adsorbs vapor onto a rotating material and handles cold spaces better. On Front Range winter jobs, that matters.

Cavity Drying and Why Walls Get Small Holes

A wall cavity is a closed pocket. Air movers in the room cannot reach it, so moisture can stay behind paint that looks fine. Injectidry style systems drill small holes, often at the base behind baseboards or under cabinet toe-kicks, and push warm dry air through the cavity.

Those small openings are patched during repair. Soft or stained drywall still comes out instead.

Moisture Mapping, Targets and Logs

1

Take baseline readings

Meters are checked on dry materials in an unaffected area to set the goal for each material.

2

Map the wet area

Thermal imaging finds cool, damp patches and pin and pinless meters confirm them.

3

Set equipment

Equipment goes where the map shows moisture, sized to the room.

4

Read and log

Each visit records temperature, humidity, grains per pound and material readings.

5

Adjust

Equipment is moved, added or taken away as the readings change.

Targets differ by material. Wood, drywall and concrete read on different scales, so a single number is never the goal. A reading is compared with the dry reference taken from the same type of material in the same home.

Monitoring Visits and When Drying Is Finished

Drying is not time-based. A crew returns, usually daily at first, to take readings and change the setup. The log shows a curve that should fall toward the dry reference.

  • Material readings are at or near the dry reference for that material.
  • Air readings inside match the unaffected area.
  • No cold spots show on thermal imaging.
  • The final visit is recorded with date and values.

Equipment is removed only after those checks. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference for this vocabulary. We name it as a standard, not a credential, and a log you can read is a fair request.

Structural Drying FAQs

Is emergency structural drying available in Wheat Ridge?

Yes. We answer around the clock and crews go out in call order. An arrival time cannot be promised, and we will explain what to do until one reaches you.

Why does my house need dehumidifiers if the air here is dry?

A closed house traps vapor from wet materials. Dehumidifiers remove it so the walls and floors can keep releasing moisture.

What is the difference between LGR and desiccant dehumidifiers?

LGR units condense vapor and work well in warm, humid rooms. Desiccant units adsorb vapor and work better in cold or very dry conditions.

Will you drill holes in my walls?

Sometimes, small ones at the base of the wall, so dry air can reach a closed cavity. They are patched in repair. Drywall that is soft or stained is removed instead.

How do I know drying is finished?

The log shows material readings at the dry reference, air readings matching an unaffected area and no cold spots on imaging. Ask to see the final reading sheet.
