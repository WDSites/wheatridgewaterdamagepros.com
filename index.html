What We Do

Water Damage Restoration, Done in the Right Order

Water damage restoration is the work of stopping the water, removing what is wet, drying the structure and documenting the loss so repairs can start on dry material.

In Wheat Ridge that usually means a basement that took on water, a ceiling stained by a roof leak, a supply line that let go behind a wall, or a sewer line that backed up through a floor drain. The order matters, because wet drywall and framing can start growing mold if the drying is delayed, and the EPA's mold guidance gives only a day or two to get things dry.

We run the job in four stages: emergency response, extraction and drying, cleanup and sanitizing, and repair. Each stage has its own page below, so you can read about the one you are standing in right now. If you are not sure which stage you need, call the 24/7 line and describe what you see. The dispatcher will sort the situation and tell you what to do while you wait.

Why Wheat Ridge Floods and Leaks

What Puts Wheat Ridge Properties at Risk

Wheat Ridge maps three floodplains inside city limits. The biggest belongs to Clear Creek, which crosses the north end of town through the Greenbelt. Lena Gulch comes in from the southwest and meets Clear Creek near Kipling. The third trails 26th Avenue from Wadsworth to Sheridan, and there the city notes that storm sewers and streets carry most of the water.

The practical point is that you do not have to live beside a creek to get water. The county itself cautions that floods often happen beyond the mapped zones, and street-level runoff along a corridor like Wadsworth Boulevard can find a window well just as easily. A basement near 26th Avenue and a house near the Greenbelt face different water paths, so we ask where the water entered before we pick equipment.

Hail adds a second path. In the May 2017 storm, National Weather Service reports put 1.75 inch stones in both Golden and Wheat Ridge, and hail-bruised roofs are a common reason a ceiling starts to stain months later. If you notice a brown ring on a ceiling after a storm, treat it as an active leak and call before the drywall softens.

Then there is plumbing and sewers. Several water districts serve the city, including Consolidated Mutual, Valley Water, Denver Water and the Wheat Ridge Water District, and wastewater in the northwest part of the city is collected by the Fruitdale Sanitation District. Knowing which provider serves your address tells you who to call to shut off or check the supply at the street.

Before the Crew Arrives

What to Do While Water Is Still Spreading

Stop the source

Close the main valve when a supply line is the source. Do not wade into water that may touch outlets or an electrical panel.

Protect people first

Switch off power to wet rooms only when the panel itself is dry and close at hand. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and phone the utility or 911 first.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, small furniture and electronics out of the wet area. Leave heavy furniture where it stands.

Document everything

Take wide and close photos and a short video before anything is moved. Start a list of damaged items for your insurer.

Keep children and pets out of any water that came from a toilet, a sewer line or a creek. That water carries contamination, and it needs a different cleanup than a clean supply-line leak. If you are unsure, assume it is contaminated and call.

Water Categories

Why the Source of the Water Changes the Job

The published industry standard for water damage restoration, ANSI/IICRC S500, sorts water by how contaminated it is. The category decides what can be dried in place and what must be removed. Ask any company you hire whether its crews work to S500.

Water categories at a glance
CategoryTypical sourceWhat it usually means for the job
Category 1Broken supply line, tub or sink overflow with clean waterDry in place when caught quickly. Porous materials may still need removal if they stayed wet too long.
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, sump pump failureCarpet pad and soaked porous material usually come out. Hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized.
Category 3Sewage, creek or storm floodwater, long-standing waterHeavy contamination. Affected porous materials are removed and the area is sanitized and cleared.

Clean water becomes worse water as it sits. A Category 1 supply-line leak left for days can behave like a Category 2 or 3 loss, which is one more reason to call early. Our page on flood damage restoration covers the contaminated end of this table in more depth.

What Drives the Cost

Cost Factors We Explain Before Work Begins

Nobody can price a water loss over the phone, and we do not guess. The price is set by what the water touched and how long it sat. We walk the property, take readings, and explain the scope and price before work starts.

Water category

Clean, gray or contaminated water changes the labor, the disposal and the sanitizing steps.

Area and depth

More rooms and more square footage mean more extraction and more drying equipment.

Materials involved

Hardwood, carpet, drywall, insulation and cabinets each dry or fail differently.

Drying time and equipment

Air movers, dehumidifiers and monitoring visits scale with the size and humidity of the loss.

Rebuild scope

Removal of wet drywall or flooring leads to repair work, which is priced separately from drying.

Time since the loss

Water left standing spreads into more materials and raises the chance of mold.

Insurance and Your Claim

Homeowners policies tend to respond to a sudden pipe failure and tend to push back on slow leaks or neglect. Flood damage is usually excluded and needs its own policy. Read your policy, then call your insurer. State guidance from the Colorado Division of Insurance is to report the loss early, keep the damage from spreading and get your insurer's instructions before repairs begin.

Questions Homeowners Ask

Wheat Ridge Water Damage FAQs

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Wheat Ridge?

Yes. Dispatch never closes. Call, describe where the water is coming from, and we will tell you which steps are safe to take while a crew is arranged. Crews go out in the order calls come in, and no arrival time is promised.

How is the price decided?

Cost follows five things: how clean the water was, how much of the building got wet, what the wet materials are, how long drying takes and how much rebuilding follows. You see the written plan and its price before any equipment is set. We do not quote exact prices by phone because the readings on site decide the work.

Will my homeowners insurance pay for this?

It depends on the policy and the cause. A burst pipe is treated differently from a slow drip, and rising flood water usually falls under a separate flood policy. We document the loss with photos and moisture readings, and your insurer decides what is covered.

How fast can mold start after a leak?

Federal mold guidance sets a one to two day window for drying wet materials. If the water has already sat longer than that, call. Our mold remediation page explains what inspection and removal involve.

Is water damage restoration the same as waterproofing?

No. Restoration dries and repairs a building after water got in, and it does not stop water from getting in again. If water keeps entering a basement, you also need a drainage or waterproofing specialist, and we will tell you so honestly.

What equipment do you use to dry a house?

Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces, and dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show where water is hiding, and extraction units remove standing water. Readings are logged each visit so drying is measured instead of guessed.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Wheat Ridge?

We serve ZIP code 80033 and the Wheat Ridge addresses around it, and we also dispatch into Arvada, Denver and Golden. If you are unsure whether your address is covered, call and give the street address.
