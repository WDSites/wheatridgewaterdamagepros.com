Why Kitchens Take Water Damage Harder Than Most Rooms

A kitchen has more water lines per square foot than any other room in the house. A supply line and a drain sit under the sink, a fitting feeds the faucet, and the cabinets around them are built to hold plates, not water. In a Wheat Ridge ranch or older brick home, a slow drip behind a cabinet can run for weeks before anyone sees it.

Most kitchen losses start small, such as a loose compression nut, a cracked drain trap or a split braided hose. Because the leak sits inside a closed cabinet, the first sign is usually a smell or a soft floor near the sink.

Dishwasher and refrigerator line failures are a kitchen problem too, but they are their own category with their own causes. Those are covered on our appliance leak water damage page. This page stays on the room itself: the cabinets, the floor and the framing under them.

Kitchen Safety Steps in the First Hour

Start with the shut-off valve. Most sinks have two small angle stops on the wall or floor under the basin, one for hot and one for cold. Turn them clockwise until they stop. If they are stuck or the leak continues, close the main water valve for the house.

Kitchens mix water with electricity. If water reached an outlet, a plugged-in dishwasher or a disposal, leave the breaker off and stay out of the standing water. For a gas smell or sparking, leave and call 911 and the gas utility from outside.

How We Work in a Wet Kitchen

1

Stop and extract

The source is isolated and standing water is pulled from the floor and the cabinet base with an extractor.

2

Map the moisture

Moisture meters and a thermal camera show how far water went under cabinets, into the toe-kicks and across the flooring.

3

Open for access

Toe-kicks come off and small holes may be drilled in cabinet bases so air can reach the cavity behind them.

4

Dry with equipment

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are placed to push dry air under and behind the cabinetry.

5

Read and record

Readings are repeated on each visit and logged until framing and subfloor reach the dry standard.

What Gets Removed and What Gets Dried

Particleboard cabinet bottoms

These swell and lose strength when wet. They are usually removed and replaced.

Solid wood and plywood boxes

These often dry in place if caught early and the finish is intact.

Toe-kick boards

Cheap to remove, and removing them opens a path for airflow behind the cabinets.

Subfloor under cabinets

Plywood usually dries. Soft or delaminated panels are cut out.

Flooring transitions

Thresholds and edges where kitchen floor meets the next room are checked for trapped moisture.

Drywall behind the sink

Cut back if it is soft. Dried in place if it is firm and readings are normal.

What Moves the Cost of a Kitchen Job

  • How long the leak ran before it was found.
  • How many linear feet of cabinets sit on wet subfloor.
  • The flooring type, since tile, vinyl and wood dry very differently.

Kitchen Mistakes That Make Damage Worse

The common one is wiping the cabinet dry and closing the door while the back panel and subfloor stay wet. If water traveled further, water mitigation covers the wider drying work.

A cabinet that stays damp can grow mold on its back panel. The mold remediation page explains next steps. Drying practice is laid out in ANSI/IICRC S500, a published reference. We also work in Arvada and Denver.

Kitchen Water Damage FAQs

Is emergency kitchen water damage restoration available in Wheat Ridge?

Yes. Our line is staffed around the clock and crews are sent in call order, with no promised arrival time. An early call puts you in line and gets you first steps for the cabinet.

Do cabinets have to come out when the sink leaks?

Not always. Solid wood and plywood often dry in place, while particleboard bottoms usually swell and get replaced. Meter readings decide, so we remove only what cannot dry.

Can the floor under my cabinets be dried without removing them?

Often yes. Toe-kicks come off and air movers push dry air into the gap. If the subfloor stays wet, a section of cabinet base may need to be opened.

What if the leak came from the dishwasher or fridge line?

The drying steps are similar, but those causes are handled on the appliance leak page. Shut off the appliance valve and call so the room can be assessed.

Will my kitchen flooring be saved?

It depends on the type and how long it was wet. Tile and sheet vinyl often survive. Hardwood and laminate are checked with meters, and ones that have cupped may need replacement.
