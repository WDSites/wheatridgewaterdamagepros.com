Commercial Water Damage Is a Coordination Job

A commercial water loss is rarely just a drying problem. It is a drying problem plus a question of who needs to be told, who owns what, and how to keep the rest of the building running. Water moves through floors and ceilings in shared structures, so a leak in one space can affect the spaces around it.

This page explains what we do on a larger loss, in plain terms. It is a call-only service. Describe the building, where the water is and whether it is still running, and dispatch will tell you what happens next. There are no forms.

What We Do on a Larger Loss

Large-area extraction

Truck-mounted and portable extractors clear standing water from wide floors and corridors.

Equipment scale-up

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are added in the number the space and readings call for.

Moisture mapping

Meters and a thermal camera trace where water went inside walls, floors and ceilings.

Drying logs

Daily readings and equipment counts are written down so owners and insurers can follow progress.

Coordination

We work with property owners and managers on access, scheduling and the scope of work.

Commercial Safety Steps in the First Hour

Start with life safety. If the building has an alarm, a sprinkler discharge or a gas smell, leave and call 911 and the utility from outside. Do not enter standing water near electrical panels, floor outlets, elevators or equipment rooms.

  • Locate the building main shut-off and close it if water supply is the source. Property managers usually know where it is.
  • Turn off power only to affected areas and only if the panel is dry and reachable.
  • Move records, electronics and stock off the floor if it is safe to do so.
  • Take photos and video of the damage before cleanup begins.

Do not use a household wet vac on large areas. Pulling water from a big floor with the wrong tool is slow and leaves moisture in carpet, pad and wall bases.

Source and safety

The supply is isolated, and hazards such as electrical exposure are identified before work starts.

3

Extract

Large extractors pull standing water, with repeated passes on carpet and pad.

4

Map and set equipment

Readings decide where air movers and dehumidifiers go, and the count is written in the log.

5

Monitor and report

Each visit records readings, equipment changes and the plan for the next day.

Drying equipment on a larger loss is added in stages. A first setup handles the area that is clearly wet. As readings show water moved farther, more equipment goes in. A log keeps that record in one place.

Working With Owners, Managers and Insurers

Commercial properties often have layers of decision makers. A property manager may hold the keys, an owner may hold the policy and a tenant may hold the contents. We keep records that make it easier for those people to talk to each other, including readings, photos and the equipment log.

We do not make coverage decisions and we do not promise that an insurer will approve a claim. Ask the insurer what the policy covers and what it needs. The state insurance division suggests an early call to the carrier and waiting for its instructions before repair work.

What Changes the Cost of a Larger Job

  • The size of the wet area and the number of floors affected.
  • Water category, with contaminated water adding removal and disposal.
  • Floor and wall materials, since carpet, concrete and framing release water at different rates.
  • How much equipment is needed and for how many days.
  • Access hours and whether crews must work around occupied space.
  • Rebuild and repair, which are scoped after drying.

A Published Reference and What We Do Not Claim

The published reference for water damage work is ANSI/IICRC S500, which defines water categories and drying goals. It is named as a reference only. We do not list industries or past clients, and we do not promise response times, since crews go out in call order.

Before the Crew Arrives

Gather the address, a contact who can unlock the building and the location of the main shut-off. If you know the water source and how long it has been running, tell dispatch. Photos taken on a phone help. Keep receipts for anything you buy to limit damage, since the insurer may ask.

Water left in a building can lead to mold, which the mold remediation page covers. Rebuilding after drying is covered under water damage repair.

Commercial Water Damage FAQs

Is emergency commercial water damage restoration available in Wheat Ridge?

Yes. The line is open day and night and crews are sent in call order, with no promised arrival time. Call when you find water.

Can you work with my property manager?

Yes. We coordinate access, scheduling and scope with owners and managers, and we keep written readings and equipment logs they can share.

Do you keep a drying log?

Yes. Moisture readings and equipment counts are recorded on each visit so progress can be checked and shared with your insurer.

Do I need to fill out a form first?

No. This is call-only. Phone dispatch, describe the building and the water, and we will explain the next step.

Can you handle sewage in a commercial space?

Sewage is Category 3 water and needs professional handling. Call and describe it, and our sewage backup cleanup page covers the general process.
