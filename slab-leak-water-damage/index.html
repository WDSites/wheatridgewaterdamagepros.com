What a Slab Leak Is and How Often It Applies Here

A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs in or under a concrete floor slab. The pipe can be a supply line carrying pressurized water or a drain line below the floor. Because the pipe is buried, the water spreads under the concrete, comes up through joints and cracks, or wicks into the flooring and walls sitting on top.

In Wheat Ridge this is the less common case. Most older homes have a basement or crawl space, so their pipes run in open joist bays where a leak is easy to see. True slab-on-grade floors turn up in additions, garages, some ranch homes and later builds. If your home is one of them, the signs below matter more.

Signs Water Is Under the Slab

Warm spot on the floor

A hot water line leaking beneath the slab warms the concrete above it.

Water meter keeps moving

With every fixture off, a meter that still turns points to a leak somewhere on the property.

Wet carpet at the slab edge

Water finds the path of least resistance and often shows along a wall or the perimeter.

Sound of running water

A faint hiss or rush with taps closed suggests pressurized water escaping.

Unexplained high water use

A bill that climbs with no change in habits can be an early clue.

None of these alone proves a slab leak. A running toilet or an outdoor irrigation line can move the meter too. A plumber confirms the source with leak detection equipment.

Safety Steps Before Anything Else

  • Shut off the main water valve. Know where it is before a leak, since it is the fastest way to stop the water.
  • Cut power to a wet area only if the breaker box is dry and reachable without standing in water. Otherwise call 911 or the utility.
  • A smell of gas means leaving the house and calling the gas utility from outside.
  • Keep children and pets off a floor that is wet or slippery.
  • Do not drill or chip the slab yourself. Broken concrete can hit a gas or electrical line.

Who Does What: Plumber First, Then Drying

Leak detection, pipe repair, rerouting a line through the attic and breaking into the concrete are plumber work. We do none of that. Once the line is stopped, our job is the water that is now in your floors and walls. Call both early, because drying cannot finish while a line is still leaking.

How We Dry and Clean a Slab Leak Loss

1

Map the moisture

A meter and an infrared camera follow water across the slab edge, the flooring and the base of walls.

2

Extract

Extractors pull standing water and soaked carpet or pad is lifted so the slab can breathe.

3

Remove what will not dry

Baseboards and the lower section of wet drywall are cut where they hold moisture.

4

Dry

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, with desiccant units where humidity is high, dry flooring, framing and the surface of the concrete.

5

Confirm

Concrete reads dry slowly, so we log readings on later visits before flooring goes back.

Concrete holds water far longer than drywall. Sealing a floor or laying new flooring over a slab that still carries moisture traps it. Hardwood and laminate are especially touchy, so hardwood floor water damage explains what happens to those finishes.

When It Is Not a Slab Leak

Most water on a Wheat Ridge floor is not under the slab. If you have a basement, the likely sources are supply lines, a water heater, a window well or groundwater, and basement water damage covers those. A pipe that cracked inside a wall or ceiling is a burst pipe water damage case, especially after a cold snap.

Insurance and a Hidden Leak

Insurers look at whether damage was sudden or built up over time, and a buried line makes that question harder to answer. Report the leak early and ask your insurer how it treats the cost of locating a leak under the slab. Ask about the cost of locating the leak as well as the damage it caused. We keep photographs and moisture logs, and coverage is never promised.

Preventing a Repeat

Know where your main shutoff is and test it each year. Watch the water meter during a quiet hour, and have a plumber look into any unexplained warm floor spot. Drying matters beyond comfort, because EPA mold guidance says water-damaged material should dry within about a day or two. If growth has started, see mold remediation. ANSI/IICRC S500 is cited here as a published reference, not a credential.

Is emergency slab leak water damage available in Wheat Ridge?

Yes. Dispatch never closes, and crews go out in the order calls arrive. An arrival time is not promised, though the dispatcher can explain the first steps.

Do you find and fix the leak?

No. A plumber locates and repairs the line, and a contractor handles any concrete work. We dry the floors and walls and clean up the water.

Are slab leaks common in Wheat Ridge?

Not as common as in slab-heavy regions, since many homes have basements or crawl spaces. They can still occur in additions, garages and homes built on a slab.

Can carpet on a slab be saved?

Sometimes. Clean water from a supply line caught early may allow carpet to be lifted, cleaned and dried. Pad is normally replaced, and the readings decide the rest.
