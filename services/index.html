Six Water Damage Services, Each With Its Own Job

Water damage is not one service. It is a sequence, and the right page depends on where you are in it. Standing water needs extraction, a wet structure needs drying, debris needs cleanup, and a dry structure needs rebuilding. Each page below explains one stage, so you can read about the one in front of you.

If you are in the middle of an emergency, skip the reading and call. Dispatch is answered 24/7, and the first minutes usually involve shutting off the source and staying out of unsafe water.

How the Services Fit Together

A typical loss moves through the pages in order. Extraction removes standing water, mitigation dries the structure, cleanup removes what cannot be saved, and repair rebuilds. Mold remediation joins the sequence only when moisture stayed long enough for growth to start, which the EPA says can often be prevented by drying within a day or two.

That order is why we recommend calling early. A loss that is extracted and dried in the first day usually needs much less demolition and rebuilding than the same loss left for a week.

When Each Stage Matters Most

Extraction matters most in the first hours, because every hour of standing water pushes moisture into drywall, subfloors and framing. Mitigation matters most in the first three days, when drying can still save material that would otherwise be torn out. Cleanup matters when the water was dirty, because contaminated material that stays in place keeps releasing odor and bacteria.

Repair matters once readings confirm the structure is dry, and not before. Rebuilding on damp framing seals moisture inside the wall, which is one of the most common reasons a repaired ceiling stains again. Mold remediation matters when growth has already started, and it begins with fixing the moisture source.

Not every loss needs every stage. A small clean-water spill caught in an hour might need only extraction and a drying visit, while a sewer backup in a finished basement needs all of them in sequence. We scope each job to what the readings show, and we explain the plan before work starts.

How Pricing and Insurance Work

No one can price a water loss over the phone. Five things set the price: water category, affected area, materials, drying time and rebuild scope. The written scope and price come before any work. We do not quote exact prices without seeing the property and taking readings.

Homeowners policies often respond to a sudden pipe failure, rarely to a long neglected leak, and flood damage typically needs its own policy. Read yours, ask your insurer, and keep photos and moisture records that help document the loss.

Published Standards We Reference

Two industry documents frame the work. ANSI/IICRC S500 covers professional water damage restoration and S520 covers professional mold remediation. The EPA publishes the homeowner guidance on drying and mold cleanup that several pages cite. These are named as published references only, and the site makes no claim of firm certification.

Services FAQs

Which service do I need first?

That depends on the situation. Standing water needs extraction first, a wet structure needs mitigation and a dry but damaged structure needs repair. Call and describe what you see, and dispatch will point you to the right stage.

Do you do plumbing repairs?

No. A licensed plumber repairs the pipe or fixture, and we handle the water damage that results. We can tell you when the source needs a plumber before repair work starts.

Is restoration the same as waterproofing?

No. Restoration repairs damage after water entered. If water keeps entering, a drainage or waterproofing specialist is the right trade, and we say so honestly.
