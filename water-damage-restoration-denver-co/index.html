What Denver's Flood Handbook Tells Homeowners

The City and County of Denver publishes a Flood Protection Handbook. It names the South Platte River, Cherry Creek, Sand Creek, Weir Gulch, Lakewood Gulch, Sanderson Gulch and Bear Creek among the area's waterways. It describes major South Platte damage in 1965 and 1973 and flooding at the I-25 and Logan Street underpass in 1998.

More useful for homeowners is its insurance guidance. The handbook says federal flood insurance does not cover subsurface flooding, including groundwater seepage or sewer backup, and that it carries a 30 day waiting period. It also says a below-grade crawlspace counts as a basement for coverage and that sump pump failure and sewer backup coverage can be added to a homeowners policy through insurers.

That is a reason to read your policy before a loss, not after one. If water already came in, flood damage restoration explains how contaminated water is handled, and your insurer decides what is covered.

Denver's Sewers and Who to Call

Denver Water provides drinking water, while the Department of Transportation and Infrastructure's Wastewater Management Division maintains the sanitary sewer and storm drainage lines, which it says total more than 1,500 miles of sanitary sewer and more than 800 miles of storm drainage. Its drainage complaint line is 303-446-3658.

Keeping the two utilities straight saves time during a backup. A no-water call goes to Denver Water, and a gurgling floor drain goes to wastewater management, while a licensed plumber handles your own service line. Then we handle the water in the house.

Before We Arrive: If Water Is Still Spreading

Find the shut-off

In a condo or multi-family building, ask building management where the stack valve is.

Keep people dry

Stay away from outlets, appliances and water that touches electrical panels. Call 911 for electrical or gas risks.

Move what you can

Lift electronics, papers and small furniture off wet floors.

Document

Take photos before moving anything and call your insurer to report the loss.
