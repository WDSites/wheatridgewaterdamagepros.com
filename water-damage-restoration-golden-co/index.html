A Clear Creek Town With a Long Flood Record

Golden sits about eight miles due west of our Wheat Ridge address, at the foothills edge where Clear Creek leaves its canyon. Federal flood records for Jefferson County note that several severe floods have been recorded in Golden since 1864. Dispatch picks up day or night, and Golden calls are sent out from the Wheat Ridge base.

The same records mention the 1888 Clear Creek flood, the July 1896 cloudbursts over Clear Creek Canyon and Golden Gate Canyon that flooded Clear Creek and Tucker Gulch, a second Tucker Gulch flood in 1948 and flash flooding in 1965. They also state that no flood protection measures exist upstream of Golden on any of the streams, and that Tucker Gulch was channelized to carry the 1 percent flood.

For a homeowner, the point is simple: Golden's water history is about speed. Mountain storms send water down quickly, and a basement near Clear Creek or Tucker Gulch can take on water quickly. Read our flood damage restoration page for how that water is handled.

Seasonal Risks: Cloudbursts, Hail and Burn-Scar Runoff

Summer thunderstorms are the first risk. The 2017 hailstorm tracked from Golden toward Wheat Ridge according to NWS Boulder, which logged 1.75 inch hail in both places, and hail-damaged roofs often leak weeks later.

Wildfire adds another. The City of Golden says ash, sediment and debris can wash into Clear Creek during rain or snowmelt after a wildfire, and the city may reduce or stop its Clear Creek intake. That is mostly a water-quality issue for the city, but burned slopes also shed water faster, which is why the city is updating its wildfire protection plan.

Winter freezes are the quiet risk. The average first freeze at the Denver station falls on October 7, and a cracked line in an unheated garage or a mountain-side vacation home can run for days. Shut off the main valve at once and call dispatch.

Golden Utilities, Permits and Who to Call

The City of Golden provides both water and sewer service. Its Utilities Division staff are available 24/7 for water and sewer emergencies, and the water emergency line is 303-384-8158. Wastewater is processed at the Molson Coors treatment plant, and the city says its water comes from the Clear Creek watershed.

The Building Department is at 911 10th Street and answers at 303-384-8000. Addresses outside city limits go to the Jefferson County Building Safety Division, which matters in the foothills where Golden mailing addresses can sit outside city limits.

The city's affordable housing page notes that homes built before 1940 have the highest risk of lead or galvanized service lines, and it is inventorying pre-1940 homes. Older lines corrode from the inside, and a failed galvanized line is a common cause of a sudden leak in an older house.

Before We Arrive: If Water Is Still Spreading

Shut off the main

Close the main water valve. In older Golden homes the valve may be stiff, so open and close it once a year.

Watch for power

If water is near outlets or the panel, stay out. For electrical or gas risk call 911 or the utility before calling us.

Protect what you can

Raise small furniture and papers, and move electronics out of the wet room.

Document

Photograph the water, then call your insurer to report the loss.

Golden Water Damage FAQs

Is emergency water removal available in Golden?

We serve Golden ZIP codes 80401, the core city around downtown and Mines, and 80403, the foothills side of Golden. ZIP 80419 belongs to Jefferson County government offices and has no homes. Call with your street address if you are unsure.
