AC Leaks in a Dry Climate: What Actually Happens Here

Wheat Ridge is dry and semi-arid, and plenty of homes here never had a refrigerated central air system. Evaporative (swamp) coolers are common on older houses. Others have a furnace with an air conditioning coil on top, with the air handler sitting in an attic, a closet, a crawl space or a basement utility room. Window units and ductless mini-splits fill in the rest.

So an AC leak is not the most common water loss in the city, and we will say so plainly. When it does happen, it tends to be slow and out of sight, which is exactly why it can soak a ceiling or a closet floor before anyone notices.

The Real Causes We See Behind AC Leaks

Clogged condensate drain

A cooling coil pulls moisture from the air, and the drain line carries it away. Algae and sludge can plug that line, and the water backs up and overflows.

Tripped or rusted drain pan

The secondary pan under an attic or closet air handler can crack or rust through, letting water reach the ceiling below.

Evaporative cooler water line

Swamp coolers use a float, a supply line and a pump. A stuck float or a cracked line can overflow onto a roof, a wall or a ceiling.

Frozen evaporator coil

A dirty filter or low airflow can ice up the coil, and the meltwater overwhelms the pan.

Window and mini-split units

A unit tilted the wrong way or a blocked drain tube sends water into the wall or onto the floor.

Safety Steps Before You Touch the Unit

  • Switch off the air conditioner or cooler at the thermostat and then at the breaker. Do not reach into a wet unit or pan while it is powered.
  • If water is dripping near a ceiling light or outlet, stay clear and shut off that circuit at a dry panel. For sparking or burning smells, call 911.
  • Turn off the cooler's water supply valve if you can find it. Evaporative coolers are connected to the household water line.
  • A refrigerant smell or oily residue means a technician is needed. Do not handle it.

Fixing the unit, whether that is clearing the drain line, replacing a pan or repairing a float, is HVAC work. We handle the water that came out of it.

How We Dry Up After an AC Leak

1

Find the wet path

Meters and an infrared camera follow the water from the unit through ceilings, walls and flooring.

2

Extract and remove

Standing water is extracted. Wet insulation, soft ceiling board and soaked carpet pad are removed when they will not dry.

3

Dry the cavities

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers work on the framing, ceiling cavity and subfloor, with small access holes where air must reach a closed space.

4

Verify

Readings are repeated until materials reach a normal range.

If the air handler sits in a basement utility room, basement water damage covers the lower-level approach. Water from a washer or water heater nearby is covered under appliance leak water damage.

Insurance and Slow Leaks

Insurers often separate a sudden break from water that dripped for weeks. Let your insurer know soon and ask whether condensate overflow counts as a sudden event on your policy. Your policy decides coverage. Our records of readings and photos help, but they do not promise an outcome.

Preventing the Next Condensate Overflow

  • Have a technician flush the drain line before cooling season.
  • Replace filters so airflow does not freeze the coil.
  • Check swamp cooler floats and supply lines each spring.
  • Fit a float switch where the unit sits above finished space.

Dry within a day or two, since the EPA links lingering moisture to mold. If growth appears, mold remediation is the next step. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference for water damage work, and naming it is not a claim of certification.

AC Unit Leak Water Damage FAQs

Is emergency AC unit leak water damage available in Wheat Ridge?

Yes. Someone answers at every hour, and crews go out in call order. We do not promise an arrival time, but we can advise you on controlling the water meanwhile.

Do you repair the AC or the swamp cooler?

No. HVAC repair is separate work. We extract the water, remove what will not dry and dry the structure so the repair is not done on wet framing.

Are AC leaks common here?

Less than in humid cities, because many homes use evaporative coolers or only a furnace. When they happen, clogged drains and cooler water lines are the usual causes.

Can the leak be behind a ceiling without a stain?

Yes. Water can sit in insulation or on top of drywall for a while before it shows. Moisture meters and thermal imaging help find it.
